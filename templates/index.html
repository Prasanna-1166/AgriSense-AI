About & Methodology

How CropWise works, and where its limits are.

What AgriSense AI does

AgriSense AI combines a machine-learning crop suitability model with location-based environmental data, live/forecast weather, and optional soil-test information to help farmers in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana think through crop choice and expected yield. It is a decision-support tool, not a replacement for agronomic expertise, government advisories, or laboratory soil testing.

Crop coverage — including what's honestly missing

The recommendation and yield models are trained on the public Crop Recommendation Dataset (~2,200 rows, 22 crops). That dataset includes Rice, Maize, and Cotton from this project's priority-crop list, plus 19 other crops (pulses, fruits, plantation crops).

It does not include Chilli, Tobacco, Wheat, Sugarcane, Turmeric, or Groundnut — all economically important crops in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. Rather than approximating these with a similar crop or fabricating plausible numbers, AgriSense AI explicitly marks them "data unavailable" everywhere they'd otherwise appear (see the Crop Directory page). Real government production statistics for these crops exist (Directorate of Economics & Statistics, data.gov.in, ICRISAT district-level data) but have not yet been integrated into a validated model in this version — see Limitations below.

The crop recommendation model

A RandomForestClassifier trained on Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium, temperature, humidity, soil pH, and rainfall, achieving 99.2% accuracy on a held-out test split of the training dataset. This accuracy reflects how well the model separates the 22 crops it was trained on — it is not a claim about real-world farm outcomes.

The yield estimation model

This is a documented synthetic/estimated model, not a field-validated real-world yield dataset. No verified, freely redistributable dataset pairing these exact features with real recorded yields was available in this project's timeframe, so the yield model is trained on reference average yields per crop, scaled by how closely input conditions match that crop's statistical comfort zone, with an irrigation adjustment. Treat yield figures as general guidance only, for the 22 crops the model supports.

Soil intelligence & provenance

Soil information can come from three places, and every value shown carries its provenance:

  • USER_ENTERED — you typed a real soil-test value (e.g. from a Soil Health Card).
  • DOCUMENT_EXTRACTED — pulled from an uploaded report; always shown for your confirmation before use, never auto-trusted.
  • ESTIMATED / EXTERNAL — pH from SoilGrids/ISRIC (modeled, not measured) where available.
  • FALLBACK — a regional/climatic-zone estimate used only when nothing better is available.

Nitrogen, Phosphorus, and Potassium cannot be reliably retrieved from any free global API at field precision — GPS location alone can never directly measure your soil's exact N/P/K or pH. If no soil test is available, the system says so plainly rather than inventing one.

Weather & climate data

Live conditions and 7-day forecasts come from Open-Meteo, a free public weather API. Climate averages use a recent 90-day archive as a practical proxy for typical conditions.

Risk assessment methodology

Risk labels (Low / Moderate / High / Data unavailable) come from documented, inspectable rules comparing forecast rainfall/temperature against fixed thresholds — not a calibrated probability-of-failure model. Each risk item states the exact rule that produced it.

Regional data reality check

Andhra Pradesh/Telangana district-level government crop statistics (Directorate of Economics & Statistics, data.gov.in, ICRISAT District Level Data) were researched for this project and are real, legitimate, well-documented sources — but they are distributed through interactive query portals rather than simple bulk downloads, and integrating them into a harmonized, leakage-free, time-aware training pipeline is a substantial data-engineering effort beyond this version's scope. This version does not fabricate a regional-evidence layer to compensate; it is listed under Limitations and Future Work instead.

Data quality vs. ML accuracy

"Data confidence" reflects how much of your input was measured, retrieved, or estimated — it says nothing about the ML model's own accuracy. A high-confidence input can still receive a lower-suitability recommendation, and vice versa.
